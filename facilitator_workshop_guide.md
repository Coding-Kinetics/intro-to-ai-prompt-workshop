# Facilitator Guide: Hands-On AI Workshop for Chicago Library Patrons
**Audience:** Adult library patrons, local club organizers, small business owners, career changers  
**Duration:** 2 Hours (120 Minutes)  
**Location:** Library Computer Lab or Community Room (with laptops / Wi-Fi)

---

## Workshop Objectives
By the end of this 120-minute session, attendees will:
1. Understand what AI is (and is not) without confusing jargon.
2. Apply the **"Kitchen Table Test"** to protect privacy and confidential data.
3. Use the **3-Part Prompt Formula (Role, Goal, Guardrails)**.
4. Complete one practical project from 4 "Choose Your Own Adventure" tracks.
5. Publish their project as a live, shareable document on GitHub using only a web browser.

---

## Detailed 120-Minute Run-of-Show

| Time | Duration | Segment | Facilitator Actions & Talking Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0:00 – 0:10** | 10 min | **Welcome & Demystifying AI** | • Welcome patrons and frame AI as an eager intern or word calculator, not magic.<br>• Run a 60-second live demo showing a vague prompt ("Write about my club") vs. a clear prompt ("Act as a neighborhood organizer..."). |
| **0:10 – 0:25** | 15 min | **The "Kitchen Table" Privacy Rule** | • The Golden Rule: *If you wouldn't leave it on a library table, don't paste it into AI.*<br>• Review Red, Yellow, and Green lights.<br>• Introduce the "Bracket Swap" trick (`[Client Name]`, `[Phone]`).<br>• Address AI hallucinations: AI generates fluent sentences, not certified facts. |
| **0:25 – 0:40** | 15 min | **The 3-Part Prompt Formula** | • Introduce **Role + Goal + Guardrails**.<br>• Build an example together on the room projector using a local Chicago reference (e.g., preparing a block party announcement or community garden flyer). |
| **0:40 – 0:45** | 5 min | **Choosing an Adventure** | • Introduce the 4 Tracks:<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;1. Community / Video Script<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;2. Career / Resume Skills Matrix<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;3. 4-Week Self-Paced Learning Roadmap<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;4. One-Click Backup Automation Script<br>• Have each patron pick one track on their handout. |
| **0:45 – 1:25** | 40 min | **Hands-On Drafting & Iteration** | • Patrons enter their prompts and generate their first draft.<br>• Circulate the room to troubleshoot logins and wording.<br>• Introduce the **Second Draft Rule**: Everyone must ask AI to refine their draft at least once (e.g., cut fluff, adjust tone, add missing details). |
| **1:25 – 1:45** | 20 min | **GitHub Browser Publishing** | • Guide everyone to GitHub.com.<br>• Create a new repository and paste their AI output into `README.md`.<br>• Show them how Markdown headings (`#`), bolding (`**`), and checkboxes (`[ ]`) format cleanly in the Preview tab.<br>• Click **Commit changes**. |
| **1:45 – 1:55** | 10 min | **Show & Tell** | • Invite 2–3 volunteers to project or read their completed output.<br>• Highlight different use cases (e.g., someone saving a backup script vs. someone polishing a resume). |
| **1:55 – 2:00** | 5 min | **Wrap-Up & Library Resources** | • Point patrons to ongoing library digital literacy programs, study spaces, and upcoming tech workshops.<br>• Remind them that their GitHub link stays active and editable from any device. |

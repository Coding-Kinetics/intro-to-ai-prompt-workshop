## Part 1: Safe & Mindful AI Rules

### The "Kitchen Table" Privacy Test
> **If you wouldn't leave a paper with this information on a public library table while grabbing a coffee, DO NOT paste it into an AI tool.**

- 🛑 **NEVER Share:** Social Security numbers, bank details, passwords, home addresses, or private medical details.
- 🟡 **The Bracket Swap Trick:** Swap real names or private details with bracketed placeholders before prompting:  
  *Instead of:* "Email Carlos Ramos at 773-555-0144 about his unpaid snow removal bill."  
  *Use:* "Email **[Client Name]** at **[Phone Number]** about an overdue invoice for **[Service]**."
- 🟢 **Always Verify:** AI is a creative writing assistant, not a legal or medical database. Double-check bus routes, dates, and phone numbers yourself.

### 🌱 Green Prompting: Caring for the Digital Footprint
Every AI query uses computing power, electricity, and cooling water at data centers. Being a responsible digital citizen is easy with a few mindful habits:

1. **Stick to Text Over Media:** Text generation uses a tiny fraction of the electricity required for image or video generators. Avoid "novelty" image prompts (like making a cartoon animal) if you only need clear words or an outline.
2. **Measure Twice, Prompt Once:** A clear, detailed prompt with your constraints on turn one cuts down on 4 or 5 back-and-forth "guessing" queries.
3. **Save and Reuse (Don't Re-Prompt):** Save your output directly to GitHub or your computer so you don't have to ask the AI to re-generate the same text next week.
4. **Choose Lightweight/Standard Models:** For drafting, checklists, or proofreading, standard or "mini/flash" models do the job with significantly lower energy draw.

## Part 2: The 3-Part Prompt Recipe

Whenever you ask AI for help, give it three ingredients:
1. **ROLE:** Who should the AI pretend to be? (*"Act as an experienced Chicago community organizer..."*)
2. **GOAL:** What exact document or list do you need? (*"...write a 3-paragraph meeting invitation for our book club."*)
3. **GUARDRAILS:** What are the limits? (*"Keep it under 150 words, friendly in tone, and mention that beginners are welcome."*)

## Part 3: Choose Your Adventure Track

Pick **ONE** track below to build today. Fill in the bracketed blanks `[...]` in your prompt box.

---

### Track 1: The Community Voice & Event Script
**Best for:** Block clubs, parent groups, book clubs, neighborhood associations, and market vendors.

> **Copy & Fill Out This Prompt:**  
> *"Act as an engaging, warm Chicago community coordinator. I run a local group called **[Name of Group, e.g., McKinley Park Gardeners / Logan Square Crafters]**. We are hosting a meeting/event about **[Event Topic/Purpose]** on **[Date/Time]** at **[Location]**.*  
>  
> *Write:*  
> *1. A 2-minute welcoming spoken opening script for me to read aloud to break the ice and acknowledge everyone braving the Chicago weather/transit.*  
> *2. Ground rules for keeping the meeting productive and welcoming.*  
> *3. A clear agenda outline with speaker cues and time marks in brackets like `[0:00 - 0:05]`.*  
>  
> *Format this in clean Markdown with bold speaker instructions."*

---

### Track 2: The Career Builder (Resume & Skills Matrix)
**Best for:** Career changers, job applicants, volunteers, or anyone returning to the workforce.

> **Copy & Fill Out This Prompt:**  
> *"Act as an encouraging professional career coach. I am preparing my resume for a role in **[Target Job or Field, e.g., administrative assistant / hospital coordinator / local non-profit aide]**.*  
>  
> *Here is an unorganized list of things I have done through work, caregiving, volunteering, or running a club:*  
> *• **[Add rough bullet point 1]***  
> *• **[Add rough bullet point 2]***  
> *• **[Add rough bullet point 3]***  
>  
> *Please write:*  
> *1. A 3-sentence professional summary for the top of my resume.*  
> *2. 4 polished, accomplishment-focused resume bullet points starting with strong action verbs that show reliability and problem-solving without corporate jargon.*  
>  
> *Format the response in clean Markdown."*

---

### Track 3: The 4-Week Personal Learning Roadmap
**Best for:** Anyone learning a new skill (Excel, bookkeeping, Spanish, GED/exam prep) self-paced at the library.

> **Copy & Fill Out This Prompt:**  
> *"Act as a patient, encouraging tutor. I want to build a self-study plan to learn **[Skill, e.g., basic Excel for office jobs / beginner conversational Spanish / fundamentals of bookkeeping]**.*  
>  
> *I can dedicate 45 minutes a day, 3 days a week for 4 weeks.*  
>  
> *Create a structured 4-week study plan in Markdown:*  
> *• Break down each week with checkable task boxes using `- [ ]`.*  
> *• Include 2 realistic practice activities for each week using free public resources.*  
> *• Include 1 quick self-check question at the end of each week so I can test my progress.*  
>  
> *Keep the pacing realistic and free of overwhelming technical terms."*

---

### Track 4: The One-Click Backup Automation Tool
**Best for:** Small business owners, writers, and club secretaries who want to safely back up their files without paying for expensive software.

> **Copy & Fill Out This Prompt:**  
> *"Act as a patient beginner IT coach. I want a simple, one-click script for my **[Windows PC OR Mac]** that creates a safe backup copy of my important file.*  
>  
> *File details:*  
> *• Source file name: `club-roster.xlsx` (located on my Desktop)*  
> *• Destination folder: A folder called `Backups` on my Desktop*  
>  
> *Requirements:*  
> *1. Provide the exact short script to paste into **[Notepad for Windows / TextEdit for Mac]**.*  
> *2. Make the script automatically attach today's date to the backed-up file name so old versions are never overwritten.*  
> *3. Explain in plain English what each line of the command does.*  
> *4. Give clear, click-by-click instructions on how to save it and run it by double-clicking."*

## Part 4: The "Second Draft" Follow-Up

Never stop at the first AI response! Enter one of these follow-ups before you save:
- **To shorten:** *"Make this 30% shorter and sound more natural and conversational."*
- **To review:** *"What is one important piece of information or risk I forgot to consider in this draft?"*
- **To reformat:** *"Convert this section into a clear bulleted checklist."*
